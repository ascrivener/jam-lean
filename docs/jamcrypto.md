# JamCrypto: the crypto parameter in plain Lean

The JAM model takes its cryptography as a parameter (`JAM.Crypto`; standing decision "Crypto stays a
parameter"). `JamCrypto` implements that parameter in plain Lean, with core and Std only. It was moved
here from jam-lean-target (main 431a9b6) on 2026-11-25, with Adam's approval. The JAM model never
imports it.

## Layout

* `JamCrypto.lean`, `JamCrypto/*.lean` (21 files), and `JamCrypto/Fast/` (see "Fast implementations"). Twenty were copied verbatim, except for the
  path of the SRS in two docstrings, and have changed since (the v0.8.1 reading switch); `Reading`
  is new. The only imports from outside JamCrypto are `JAM.Crypto`, `JAM.Sha512`,
  `JAM.Keccak` and `JAM.Blake2b`.
  * Ed25519 (`Ed25519`, `Ed25519Sign`).
  * Bandersnatch IETF VRF (`Bandersnatch`, `BandersnatchSign`), Thin VRF (`ThinVrf`) and Pedersen
    VRF (`PedersenVrf`).
  * The ring root and ring VRF (`Ring*`), over BLS12-381 (`Bls12381*`).
  * Development keys (`DevKeys`) and the `JAM.Crypto` instance (`Crypto`).
  * The reading of the v0.8.1 path's spec, Draft 35 (`Reading`: `.prose`, the default, or `.vectors`).
* `JamCryptoTest.lean` (`cryptotest`) and its vectors `JamCryptoTest/vectors/` (see their README).
  The provenance generator is in `JamCryptoTest/generator/` and is not built.
* `RingTest.lean` (`ringtest`) and its vectors `JamCryptoTest/ring/*.txt` (`tests/vectors/` in the
  target). The generators are in `JamCryptoTest/ring/gen/` and are not built.
* `JamCryptoTest/data/bls12-381-srs-2-11-uncompressed-zcash.bin`, the ring VRF's KZG SRS (see
  `JamCryptoTest/data/README.md`).
  * 590320 octets, sha256 `1d7d27e4f5f3c6190989bea58803180d3e19f725a57069392a405ac78b233c7d`.
  * Byte-identical to `assets/srs/zcash-srs-2-11-uncompressed.bin` of davxy/bandersnatch-vrf-spec
    (checked 2026-11-25).

Run from the repository root:

```
lake build JamCrypto
lake build cryptotest && .lake/build/bin/cryptotest     # about 30 s (with the differential tests)
lake build ringtest && .lake/build/bin/ringtest         # about 35 s
```

CI's `model` job runs both. `scripts/CheckAxiomsModel.lean` checks that JamCrypto, too, uses only
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Fast implementations (`JamCrypto.Fast`)

The reference implementations above compute on `Nat` (GMP). `JamCrypto/Fast/` computes the same
functions on fixed limbs, and **each fast function is proved equal to its reference** (Adam,
2026-11-26: no tested-only equalities; reference implementations unchanged; spec modules never
import Fast modules).

* `Fast/Fr.lean`, `Fast/Fp25519.lean` (generated by `Fast/gen/field.py`): 𝔽_r of BLS12-381 (the
  Bandersnatch base field) and 𝔽_(2^255−19) as Montgomery forms on nine 29-bit limbs in `UInt64`s,
  with fully unrolled product-scanning multiplication and squaring. The generator also emits the
  proofs: `mul_spec`, `sq_spec`, `add_spec`, `sub_spec` (each keeps the limbs reduced and computes the
  `Nat` operation mod p on Montgomery values), then the value lemmas (`value_mul`, …, `value_ofNat`,
  `toNat_eq`, `beq_eq`, `isZero_eq`) and a `LawfulField` instance. Generic lemmas are in
  `Fast/Limbs.lean`; the interface is `Fast/FieldClass.lean`. Each field module takes about
  3.5 min and 3.8 GB to check. (`Fq`, BLS12-381's base field on 14 limbs, is not generated yet: its
  proofs need more memory; it is for the ring.)
* `Fast/Sim.lean`: loops run on related states stay related (`forIn_range_rel`).
* `Fast/EdwardsF.lean`: twisted Edwards curves over any lawful fast field, each operation the same
  sequence of field operations as `JamCrypto.Curve`/`Modular` and proved to correspond: `add`,
  `neg`, `sub`, `smul` (double-and-add), `eq`, `isZero`, `valid`, `toAffine`, `powMod`, `invMod`,
  `isSquare`, `sqrtTS`.
* `Fast/Bandersnatch.lean`: `ietfVerify_eq`, `vrfOutput_eq`, `Thin.verify_eq`, `Thin.vrfOutput_eq`.
* `Fast/Ed25519.lean`: `verifyWith_eq` (all three `Rules`), `verify_eq`.
* `Fast/Crypto.lean`: `Fast.withSignatures` and `withSignatures_eq : Fast.withSignatures … =
  JamCrypto.withSignatures …` — what executables use in place of `withSignatures`.

**Hash to curve stays on the reference.** `Bandersnatch.elligator2` destructures a pair from an
`if` (`let (x, odd) := if isSquare … then … else …`); the kernel cannot check any unfolding of its
body (it reduces `isSquare` on symbolic arguments and runs out of memory; even the equation lemma
`elligator2.eq_def` does), so the fast verifiers call the reference `hashToCurve` and convert its
point (exact: its coordinates are reduced, `red_smul`). Changing the reference to two plain `let`s
would remove this limit; that is Adam's decision (pending).

Proof-engineering notes for further work in these files: the kernel reduces matches on non-constructor
discriminants and `if`s through their `Decidable` instances when a cast compares differently shaped
terms, which evaluates `powMod`-style loops with literal bounds on symbolic data. So: generalize every
match discriminant before touching `let`s; prefer `rw`, `extract_lets` and lemma applications to
`simp`/`dsimp` casts; and keep the reference's exponentiations folded (`attribute [local irreducible]`).

`JamCryptoTest/Fast.lean` (run by `cryptotest`) still compares fast and reference functions on all
vectors and randomized inputs, as an extra check.

Timings (cryptotest, per valid signature, this container): Ed25519 1.83 ms → 0.29 ms, IETF VRF
12.7 ms → 1.7 ms, Thin VRF 12–17 ms → 2.0 ms.

## Derivation

Nothing is ported from an implementation. Each part is written from its specification, and the
published test vectors are the reference:

* **Ed25519:** RFC 8032, with ZIP 215's acceptance rules.
* **Bandersnatch VRFs:** the Bandersnatch VRF-AD specification.
  * The v0.8.0 path follows the draft GP v0.8.0 cites (Draft 29), read together with ark-vrf
    0.1.0's vectors, which are the draft's own.
  * The v0.8.1 path follows Draft 35 (commit 181aba2), the draft GP v0.8.1 cites.
* **Ring proof:** eprint 2023/002.
* **Hashing to the curve:** RFC 9380.

Where a spec's prose and its vectors disagree, the v0.8.0 path follows the vectors (it stays as
validated against the 0.8.0 data). The v0.8.1 path follows the prose of the cited Draft 35 by
default and the vectors on request, through one switch, `JamCrypto.Reading` (Adam, 2026-11-25; see
"The two readings of Draft 35" below). Each case is listed below.


Points where the GP v0.8.0 leaves the cryptography to its references, and where the model and the
references disagree. One section per point.

## Ring root: the ring size is the validator count V, not |k| (GP ambiguity)

GP Appendix G defines O(**k**) = commit(**k**) and defers commit to the Bandersnatch ring VRF.
The ring proof's polynomial domain depends on a *ring size*, which the GP does not fix: ark-vrf
sizes the domain as the least power of two ≥ ring size + 257 (512 for 6, 2048 for 1023).

The 0.8.0 vectors fix it at the chain's V, whatever |γ_P'| is: in jam-test-vectors 0.8.0
`stf/safrole/full/enact-epoch-change-with-set-resize-1.json`, γ_P' holds 6 keys and its γ_Z' is the
commitment over the domain of 2048 (ring size 1023), not 512. `JamCrypto.Ring.root srs V keys`
takes V explicitly; `JamCryptoTest/ring/ring-root-jam.txt` includes that case.

## Context strings: `$jam_…` is notation, not octets (model issue in jam-lean)

The GP writes contexts as `$jam_ticket_seal`, `$jam_valid`, …; the `$` marks a string token and is
not part of the octets. Ring VRF tickets in jam-test-vectors 0.8.0 (`stf/safrole`) verify with the
context "jam_ticket_seal" ⌢ η'_2 ⌢ attempt, and fail with a leading `$`
(`JamCryptoTest/ring/ring-ticket-jam.txt`, checked by `ringtest`).

jam-lean's `JAM.contextString "$jam_ticket_seal"` (Safrole.lean) keeps the `$`, as do `Xvalid`,
`Xinvalid`, `Xguarantee` (Disputes.lean) and `Xavailable` (Assurances.lean). jam-lean #196 has
since dropped the `$` from these context strings. `JamCrypto.Ring.verify` takes the context as given.

The same holds for the Bandersnatch IETF VRF and Ed25519 contexts: in the 0.8.0 `no_forks` session
and the 0.8.0 fuzz-report traces, every seal (`jam_fallback_seal` ⌢ η'_3), entropy source
(`jam_entropy` ⌢ Y(H_S)) and Ed25519 assurance, guarantee and judgment signature verifies with the
context octets without `$` (`JamCrypto.Bandersnatch.ietfVerify`, `JamCrypto.Ed25519.verify`, and
ark-vrf 0.1.0 / ed25519-dalek), and none with it; the one IETF signature that fails is in a trace
step polkajam also rejects.

## Ed25519 acceptance rules: ZIP 215 (GP leaves it open; Adam's decision 2026-11-15)

GP v0.8.0 (notation.tex) defines 𝕍̄_k⟨m⟩ as "the set of valid Ed25519 signatures, defined by
[RFC 8032]". RFC 8032 §5.1.7 allows the cofactored check [8][S]B = [8]R + [8][k]A or the
cofactorless [S]B = R + [k]A, and its §5.1.3 decoding rejects non-canonical encodings, which
widely used implementations accept. The jam-fuzzing consensus vectors (commit 5bbd4266,
`crypto/ed25519`, removed by #219) target ZIP 215: small-order and non-canonically encoded A and R
with s = 0, all valid. `JamCrypto.Ed25519.verify` follows ZIP 215; the RFC 8032 strict variants
are parameters (`Ed25519.Rules`), and `JamCryptoTest/vectors/ed25519.txt` gives each vector's
verdict under all three.

## Bandersnatch IETF VRF: the cited spec draft's prose disagrees with its vectors

GP v0.8.0 Appendix G cites the Bandersnatch VRF-AD specification, "Draft 29" (biblio.bib,
`hosseini2024bandersnatch`). Draft 29 (davxy/bandersnatch-vrf-spec, commit 01b0eb0, §2.1) says
`cLen` = 16 and that `string_to_int` reads little-endian. A 16-octet challenge would make the
signature 32 + 16 + 32 = 80 octets, but GP (bandersnatch.tex) has 𝔹̄_k⟨c⟩⟨m⟩ ⊂ 𝕐_96. Draft 29's
own IETF test vectors are those of ark-vrf 0.1.0: a 32-octet c, derived as the first 32 octets of
the SHA-512 challenge hash read big-endian, reduced mod r, stored little-endian (ark-vrf 0.1.0
`utils::challenge_rfc_9381`). Draft 28 (commit 38cf32d) still says `cLen` = 32. `JamCrypto.Bandersnatch`
follows the vectors and GP's 96 octets. One further detail comes from arkworks 0.5 rather than from
RFC 9380, which the spec cites: `expand_message_xmd` pads with 48 zero octets instead of SHA-512's
block size of 128. (The Elligator 2 sign rule, y odd for the first candidate x and even for the
second, is RFC 9380's own: §6.7.1 steps 6 and 7, sgn0(y) = 1 and 0. An earlier version of this
page listed it as a deviation; it is not one. Z = 5 is also RFC 9380's choice, App. H.3, and the
map to the Edwards curve is its App. D.1.) The fuzzer's blocks verify with this.

## GP v0.8.1 (GP #550): Thin VRF and the VRF spec's Draft 35

GP v0.8.1 switches 𝔹̄ to the **Thin VRF** and cites the Bandersnatch VRF-AD specification
**Draft 35** (davxy/bandersnatch-vrf-spec commit 181aba2) for all of Appendix G, so the ring VRF
changes too. The v0.8.1 path is chosen by `JamCrypto.GpVersion.v081`; the default is v0.8.0. (jam-lean-target
selects it with `--gp 0.8.1` or `JAM_GP=0.8.1`.)

* `JamCrypto/ThinVrf.lean`: suite `Bandersnatch-SHA512-ELL2-v1`, SHA-512 counter-mode transcript,
  hash to curve (DST suite_id ⌢ 0x60), output hash, Thin VRF prove and verify. Tests: the spec's 7
  thin vectors (`JamCryptoTest/vectors/bandersnatch_thin.txt`).
* `JamCrypto/PedersenVrf.lean`: the Draft 35 Pedersen VRF. Tests: the spec's 7 pedersen vectors.
* `JamCrypto/RingVerify081.lean`, and `Ring.Consts.v081` for the ring root: compared with v0.8.0,
  the ring proof (SNARK) and the ring layout are unchanged. The constants (blinding base,
  padding point, accumulator seed; each is hash_to_curve of its name, which cryptotest checks)
  and the ring transcript's label (the new suite id) are new, and so are the Pedersen proof and
  the output hash. Tests: the spec's 7 ring vectors, roots and proofs (`JamCryptoTest/ring/ring-d35.txt`).

Choices the paper and the draft leave open:

* **96-octet signature layout.** GP gives 𝔹̄ as 96-octet strings and reads Y from the signature,
  but the draft encodes only the proof (R, s). We read it as O ⌢ R ⌢ s, the output point first as
  in the v0.8.0 IETF signature and the ring proof (`Thin.thinEncode`/`thinDecode`); Adam,
  2026-11-25. The gap is raised upstream as gavofyork/graypaper#593. The planned check: once fuzzer
  blocks for 0.8.1 exist, only one layout will verify.
* **Z_pad**, the fixed points and the ring transcript label: the draft's prose and its vectors
  disagree; see the next section.
* **Ring domain**: §5 of Draft 35 fixes the domain at 2048. The 181aba2 ring vectors themselves
  use domain 512; the spec's later commit 0b7422f regenerates them at 2048, and our root matches
  that regeneration too. Adam, 2026-11-25: the v0.8.1 path uses the fixed domain 2048 of the cited
  §5 for every chain, tiny included (`Ring.domain081`, a parameter of `Ring.crypto`), as the
  reading of the cited spec, to be confirmed by 0.8.1 vectors or fuzzer blocks. The v0.8.0 path
  stays sized by V.

## The two readings of Draft 35 (`JamCrypto.Reading`)

Adam, 2026-11-25 (option A): on the v0.8.1 path the cited spec's **prose** is the default reading,
and the reading of its **vectors** is an explicit switch. `JamCrypto.Reading` is `.prose` or
`.vectors`; `Thin.hashToCurve`, `Thin.verify`, `Thin.sign`, `Pedersen.prove`/`verify`,
`Ring.Consts.v081`, `Ring.verify081`, `Ring.crypto` and `withSignatures` take it as their last
argument, default `.prose`. The v0.8.0 path has no reading. (jam-lean-target will select
`.vectors` as a listed assumption.)

| Point | Draft 35 prose (`.prose`, default) | Draft 35 vectors (`.vectors`) |
|---|---|---|
| Z_pad of expand_message_xmd (App. A.2) | 128 zero octets | 48 zero octets |
| Blinding base B (§4), accumulator base S and padding point □ (§5) | hash_to_curve of "pedersen-blinding", "ring-accumulator", "ring-padding" with the prose hash to curve | the printed coordinates (the same names with the 48-octet Z_pad) |
| Ring proof transcript label (§5) | "ring-proof" | `Bandersnatch-SHA512-ELL2-v1` |

The disagreements, in the draft's words:

* **Z_pad.** App. A.2: "using the method defined in section 3 of [RFC-9380], with the *Elligator 2*
  map to curve (section 6.8.2) and `expand_message_xmd` with SHA-512 (section 5.3.1)". RFC 9380
  §5.3.1 step 4: "Z_pad = I2OSP(0, s_in_bytes)", and s_in_bytes is 128 for SHA-512 ("s = 1024 for
  SHA-512"). The vectors (every input point h, hence every output, signature and proof) need 48.
  Draft 36 (commit fcf7012, after the tag GP v0.8.1 cites) adds: "With one difference from section
  5.3.1: the zero prefix Z_pad … has L = 48 octets … An implementation with the RFC prefix does not
  reproduce the constants of this document or the vectors of Appendix C." We do not follow Draft 36
  (it is not what GP v0.8.1 cites). The rest of A.2 matches RFC 9380 as written (Z = 5 by App. H.3,
  the sign rule of §6.7.1, the rational map of App. D.1, cofactor 4, 48 octets per field element),
  so Z_pad is the only difference in the hash to curve.
* **The fixed points.** §4: "The *blinding base* B ∈ G is defined as: x = 2333…5889, y = 5552…9963.
  A point with unknown discrete logarithm derived using the `hash_to_curve` function as described
  in Appendix A.2 with input the string: "pedersen-blinding"." §5 says the same of the accumulator
  base S ("ring-accumulator") and the padding point □ ("ring-padding"). The printed coordinates are
  the derivation with the 48-octet Z_pad, so under the prose reading of A.2 the two halves of each
  definition disagree. `.prose` takes the derivation (the points are recomputed with the prose hash
  to curve, once, as closed constants); `.vectors` takes the printed coordinates.
* **Ring transcript label.** §5, Fiat–Shamir transform: "Begin with empty transcript and
  "ring-proof" label." Every ring vector of Draft 35 (`JamCryptoTest/ring/ring-d35.txt`) verifies
  only with the suite id `Bandersnatch-SHA512-ELL2-v1` as the label.
* **Not modelled under either reading.** §5 also says "Push R to the transcript after
  instancing." R is not defined in §5 (in §4 R is the Pedersen proof's nonce commitment), and the
  draft gives no label or encoding for it. The vectors' transcript pushes nothing after the
  instance Ȳ other than the proof's own columns, and we follow that under both readings: `.prose`
  therefore omits this step of the prose, because the prose leaves it underdetermined. An open
  prose/vectors disagreement, listed in `docs/upstream-candidates.md`.
* The ring domain of 2048 is the same under both readings: §5 fixes it, and Adam's decision of
  2026-11-25 follows §5 (above).

**The prose reading has no external oracle.** No published vectors follow it: every Draft 35
vector (thin, pedersen, ring) checks `.vectors`, and fails under `.prose`, which `cryptotest` and
`ringtest` confirm. What is checked under `.prose`:

* its expand_message_xmd against the ten SHA-512 vectors of RFC 9380 App. K.3
  (`JamCryptoTest/vectors/expand_message_xmd_sha512.txt`) — the one external check, and only of
  that piece;
* the three derived points: they hash to the curve, lie in the prime subgroup, are not the
  identity, round-trip through the point encoding, are pairwise distinct, differ from the printed
  points, and are what `Pedersen.blindingBase` and `Ring.Consts.v081` use;
* Thin VRF sign → verify and Pedersen prove → verify round trips on the vectors' keys and inputs,
  and that another key or ad, or the `.vectors` verifier, rejects them.

Everything else under `.prose` is untested. In particular the ring VRF under `.prose` (the
"ring-proof" label, the derived accumulator and padding points in the ring proof) is not exercised
at all: JamCrypto has no ring prover, so no `.prose` ring proof exists to verify, and a `.prose`
ring root is computed but has nothing to be compared with.

## Open readings (from jam-lean-target's docs/gaps.md)

The GP v0.8.1 crypto readings are still to be confirmed by 0.8.1 vectors or fuzzer blocks:

* The 96-octet Thin VRF signature is read as O ⌢ R ⌢ s. GP gives 96 octets; the VRF spec encodes only
  R ⌢ s (Adam, 2026-11-25; raised as gavofyork/graypaper#593).
* The ring domain is fixed at 2048 for every chain, per Draft 35 §5 (Adam, 2026-11-25). v0.8.0
  stays sized by V.
* Where Draft 35's prose and vectors disagree (Z_pad, the fixed points, the ring transcript label),
  the default is the prose (`Reading.prose`) and the vectors are a switch (`Reading.vectors`); Adam,
  2026-11-25. Only 0.8.1 vectors or fuzzer blocks can say which one JAM implementations use.
